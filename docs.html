1The short version

Pakka splits a Morpho USDC vault share on Arc into two tokens: one that is worth exactly 1 USDC on a set date, and one that collects all the variable interest until then. Buying the first at a discount locks in a fixed rate.

What it is

Every lending market on Arc today pays a variable rate. Morpho might pay 5% this week and 3% next month. Pakka turns that variable stream into two tradeable parts. The principal token (PT) redeems for 1 USDC at maturity, so whoever buys it below 1 USDC knows their return from the first day. The yield token (YT) receives every bit of interest the underlying vault earns until maturity, so it suits someone who expects rates to rise. Together, one PT and one YT are always worth one unit of the underlying, which is why the system never needs an outside price feed for its own accounting.

This is the design Pendle made popular on other chains. Pendle runs on Plasma, one of Arc's direct competitors, and as far as could be found nothing like it runs on Arc.

Who it is for first

The first user Pakka is designed around is an AI agent with predictable costs. An agent that knows it will spend about 50 USDC a month on paid APIs can buy PT that matures once a month. Cash arrives exactly when each bill is due and earns a fixed rate until then. This is a bond ladder, a standard treasury technique, made callable by software through one function and an MCP tool.

Agents should not hold their owner's money freely, so the agent works inside a Tijori (Hindi for safe). It is a small contract the owner deploys. The agent can buy tickets, cash them, claim interest and pay payees the owner has approved, within caps. Money can only leave to the owner or to those payees. A leaked agent key cannot drain it.

Why Arc

On Arc, USDC is both the money and the gas. An agent never needs to hold a second, volatile token just to keep transacting, and on other chains running out of gas is a real way for a bot to stall. Morpho on Arc passed $500M in deposits within about two weeks of launch, so there is real yield to split from day one. Uniswap v4 is live for the PT market. Finality is deterministic and under a second, so an agent can cash a matured ticket and pay a bill in the same flow without waiting for confirmations.

5How Pakka works for its users

Pakka is a vending machine, not a bank manager. It never holds anyone's money on their behalf. USDC goes in, tickets come straight back to the same wallet.

Priya saves at a fixed rate

Priya has 100 USDC she will not need for three months. She opens the Pakka site, connects a wallet and sees Morpho's current variable rate next to fixed rates for each maturity. She picks 31 January, enters 100 USDC and signs once. A ticket lands in her wallet that pays a known amount on that date. On 31 January she presses Cash out and receives it in USDC. If she needs the money earlier she sells the ticket on the pool at that day's price, much like breaking a fixed deposit early.

Rahul's agent manages its own budget

Rahul runs an AI agent that spends about 50 USDC a month on paid services. Once, on the website, he creates a Tijori, deposits 150 USDC, sets the agent's address and approves the service provider as a payee with a monthly cap. From then on the agent works alone. It calls planTreasury(50, 3) through the MCP tool and receives three tickets. Each month it cashes the ticket that matured and pays the bill. Rahul can withdraw everything at any time.

Arjun buys the variable side

Arjun thinks rates will rise, so he buys YT from a small Yield tab. If Morpho's rate climbs he earns more than he paid; if it falls he earns less. This side exists because the mechanism needs it, not because it is the pitch. Show it in the demo, but do not market leverage or points.

The ladder, in numbers

At an illustrative fixed rate of 5% a year, three tickets of 50 USDC each cost:

6The mechanism

The whole system rests on one number, the index, which says how many USDC a fixed amount of vault shares is worth. Everything else is bookkeeping around it.

The index

A Morpho vault is an ERC-4626 vault. Depositors receive shares, and the shares grow in value as borrowers pay interest. Pakka reads that value directly from the vault.

INDEX_UNIT = 10 ** (shareDecimals + 18)
index      = vault.convertToAssets(INDEX_UNIT)     // USDC (raw) per INDEX_UNIT shares
pyIndex    = max(index, pyIndexStored)             // stored, never decreases

Morpho shares use 18 decimals and USDC uses 6. Querying a single whole share would return about 1,000,000 raw units and lose daily interest to rounding. Querying INDEX_UNIT shares gives at least 18 digits of precision whatever the decimals are.

Splitting and backing

split       :  s shares  ->  s * pyIndex / INDEX_UNIT   PT  and the same amount of YT
PT backing  :  totalPT * INDEX_UNIT / pyIndex            shares the contract must hold

PT and YT are counted in USDC units, not shares, so one PT is a claim on exactly one USDC of value at maturity. As the index rises, each share is worth more, so fewer shares are needed to back the same PT. The shares that are freed are the interest, and they belong to YT holders.

YT interest

interestShares = ytBalance * INDEX_UNIT * (current - previous) / (previous * current)

Each holder has their own userIndex, the index when their interest was last settled. Interest is settled for both sides before any YT balance changes: mint, burn and every transfer. Without that rule, someone could buy YT just before a claim and collect interest they never earned. The formula is computed with a single rounding step, so the contract can never owe more than it holds.

Worked example

Deposit 100 shares when the index is 1.05 USDC per share: the contract mints 105 PT and 105 YT. The index later reaches 1.06. Backing 105 PT now needs 105 / 1.06 = 99.057 shares. The contract holds 100, so 0.943 shares are surplus. At 1.06 they are worth 1.00 USDC, which is the YT holders' interest: 105 USDC of face value growing by 0.952%.

The fixed rate

impliedAPY = (1 / ptPrice) ^ (1 / yearsToMaturity) - 1

A PT priced at 0.99 USDC with three months left implies (1/0.99)4 − 1 ≈ 4.1% a year. Because one PT plus one YT is one unit of the asset, YT trades at roughly one minus the PT price. Buying the variable side is therefore three steps in one transaction: deposit USDC, split, sell the PT.

Maturity

On the first interaction at or after expiry, the contract freezes indexAtExpiry. YT stops earning at that point. PT then redeems for pt * INDEX_UNIT / indexAtExpiry shares, which may be unwrapped straight to USDC. Because that is a fixed number of shares, any yield the vault earns after maturity accrues to PT holders who have not yet cashed out. Anyone can call settleExpiry() at the moment of expiry.

When the vault loses money

If Morpho takes bad debt, the vault's index falls below the stored high-water mark. Because pyIndex never decreases, YT earns nothing until the vault recovers past that mark, and PT holders absorb the loss when they redeem. The share count stays solvent, so nobody is locked out. The honest wording for users is that the rate is fixed as long as the underlying vault stays healthy.

Rounding and the invariant

Every payout rounds down, every requirement rounds up in the contract's favour. The invariant tested after every fuzz step is that vault shares held are at least the PT backing plus every holder's accrued interest. The final fuzz phase expires the series and makes every holder claim and redeem; only rounding dust may remain.

8Arc engineering notes

Arc is EVM-compatible, but a handful of differences break naive code. Each of these has cost someone a day already.

Behaviour What to do
USDC has two interfaces sharing one balance: native (18 decimals) and ERC-20 at 0x3600…0000 (6 decimals) Use only the ERC-20 interface in contracts. Never use msg.value or payable USDC paths. In Uniswap v4, use the ERC-20 address as the pool currency, never address(0), which would be native USDC with 18 decimals.
Minimum base fee 20 gwei; transactions below it are silently dropped by the mempool Always pass a gas price, for example --with-gas-price 25gwei in Foundry, and set maxFeePerGas of at least 20 gwei in viem
The next block's base fee is published in the parent header's extraData; the base fee is paid to the block producer, not burned Fee quotes are predictable. Show them to users in USDC, not gwei.
EIP-7708: native USDC movements emit a standard ERC-20 Transfer log from a system address Indexers that count both that log and the ERC-20 log will double-count. Pick one stream. Gas deductions emit no log; read them from receipts.
Deterministic finality under a second No confirmation counts or reorg handling. A confirmed receipt is final, so the agent can cash out and pay in sequence.
Osaka baseline, including EIP-7702; ERC-4337 supported natively Tijori can later become a smart-account policy. A paymaster can cover gas for a wallet that holds only collateral.
Morpho vault shares use 18 decimals over 6-decimal USDC Read decimals() at runtime; never assume. Pakka's INDEX_UNIT handles this.
A vault may be short of withdrawable liquidity at high utilisation Offer cash-out in shares as well as USDC; check maxRedeem in the router before unwrapping.

12Submission kit

Name and one line

Pakka: fixed-rate USDC on Arc, built for agents.

Description

Pakka splits Morpho USDC vault shares on Arc into a principal token, worth exactly 1 USDC at maturity, and a yield token that collects all variable interest until then. Buying the principal token at a discount locks in a fixed rate. An AI agent with predictable costs can build a ladder of maturities in one call, so cash arrives when each bill is due, while a Tijori contract limits it to buying tickets, cashing them and paying owner-approved payees. Pendle-style fixed rates run on Plasma; Arc had none.

What it uses Arc for

Arc is the only chain where an agent holds one asset, USDC, for both spending and gas, so a treasury bot can never stall for lack of a gas token. Yield comes from Morpho vaults on Arc. Principal tokens trade in Uniswap v4 pools on Arc. Sub-second deterministic finality lets an agent cash a matured ticket and pay a bill in the same flow. All contracts are deployed and verified on Arc mainnet, with full maturity cycles completed on chain.

Glossary

PT, principal token Redeems for one unit of the asset at maturity. Bought at a discount, it earns a fixed rate.
YT, yield token Receives all of the vault's variable interest until maturity
Maturity, expiry The date a series ends: YT stops earning and PT can be redeemed
Series One YieldToken and its PT for one vault and one maturity
Index USDC value of a fixed amount of vault shares; rises as the vault earns
Implied APY The fixed annual rate implied by PT's discount and time to maturity
Bond ladder Several fixed-rate holdings maturing at staggered dates so cash arrives on schedule
Tijori Pakka's owner-controlled safe that lets an agent manage a budget within fixed rules
ERC-4626 The standard interface for yield-bearing vaults; Morpho vaults implement it
MCP Model Context Protocol, the standard way AI agents discover and call external tools
Deterministic finality Once a transaction is confirmed it can never be reversed or reordered
