01

The idea

One deposit, cut into two tickets.

USDC in a lending vault earns interest, but the rate moves every day. Pakka takes one vault deposit and cuts it into two tokens that are sold to two different people.

  • The principal token (PT) pays back the full deposit on a fixed date. It is sold today for less than that, and the gap is the buyer's fixed interest.
  • The yield token (YT) collects all the interest the vault earns on that deposit until the same date. Its buyer is betting the vault will earn more than the token cost.

Take 100 USDC for one year. The fixed buyer pays 95 and the yield buyer pays 5. Together that is the 100 that sits in the vault.

The PT holder gets 100 in every row. The vault only ever pays out what it actually holds: the 100 plus whatever it earned. Nobody promises money that is not already there, and the uncertainty about future rates is carried by the YT holder, who chose it.

The same idea exists in traditional finance. A PT behaves like a zero-coupon bond or treasury bill: bought below face value, paid in full at the end. Cutting principal and interest apart is called stripping, and swapping an uncertain rate for a certain one is what an interest rate swap does.

02

In the app

Buying and exiting.

Buy a principal token

  1. Open Rates and pick a date. Each row shows the fixed rate and the cost today per 1 USDC of face value.
  2. On Buy PT, enter the face value you want to receive at maturity and press Get quote.
  3. Review the cost, then press Buy principal token and confirm in your wallet.

Buy a yield token

  1. Connect your wallet and open Buy YT.
  2. Enter how much USDC to deposit, pick a date and press Preview.
  3. Press Buy yield token. The app deposits your USDC, splits it, and sells the principal half to the pool. That USDC comes straight back to your wallet, and you keep the yield token. The net cost is the deposit minus what came back.

Get your money out

  • Cash out a matured principal token for its full face value in USDC.
  • Take vault shares instead if the vault is short of USDC that day. The shares can be redeemed later.
  • Sell early before maturity at the pool's current price. You may get less than you paid.
  • Claim interest on a yield token whenever some has built up.
  • Merge tickets if you hold both a PT and a YT of the same series. Together they are the original deposit, so they convert back to USDC at any time before maturity.
03

Glossary

The words on the screen.

Principal tokenPT · ticket

A token that pays its face value in USDC on the maturity date. The app also calls it a ticket.

You buy it below face value. It earns no interest along the way; the discount is the interest.

Yield tokenYT

A token that collects the variable interest the vault earns on one USDC of deposit until maturity.

It returns no principal. After maturity it stops earning and is worth only the interest still unclaimed.

Face value

The USDC amount a principal token pays at maturity. A ticket with a face value of 1 USDC pays 1 USDC.

MaturityExpiry · the date

The date and time a principal token can be cashed out for its full face value, and the moment a yield token stops earning.

Series

One vault paired with one maturity date. Each series has its own PT, its own YT and its own pool.

Discount

The gap between a ticket's face value and what you pay for it today. Paying 0.95 for a face value of 1 is a discount of 0.05.

Fixed rateSimple annualized rate

The discount expressed as a yearly percentage, so tickets with different dates can be compared.

It is a simple rate, not APY: it does not assume you reinvest. It is fixed from the moment you buy.

Variable rate

What the vault is paying right now. It changes with borrowing demand and is what yield-token holders receive.

Vault

The contract that holds the USDC and earns interest on it. Every ticket is backed by a deposit in the vault.

A deployment may use a demo vault whose yield is simulated and which does not lend. The Rates page says so when it does.

Vault shares

Your claim on the vault itself. Each share is worth a little more USDC as the vault earns. Taking shares instead of USDC lets you exit even when the vault is short of cash.

Split and merge

Splitting turns one vault deposit into one PT and one YT. Merging does the reverse: one PT plus one YT of the same series becomes the deposit again.

PoolLiquidity

The on-chain market where principal tokens trade against USDC. Its price sets the fixed rate. If the pool is thin, large purchases or early sales get a worse price or cannot fill.

Quote

The exact cost for the amount and date you entered, read from the pool at that moment. A quote is valid for up to two minutes.

Slippage allowance

How much the price may move between the quote and your transaction before it is cancelled. The app allows 0.50%. Maximum spend is the quote plus that allowance; any unused USDC is refunded.

Estimated exit value

What a ticket would return if you exited right now: the pool's sale price before maturity, or the vault's payout after it.

Entry capEntry TVL

The most new USDC a series accepts. Each series is capped at 500 USDC. Existing tickets can always exit.

Safety limitCircuit breaker

If the vault's share price jumps by more than 1% at once, new purchases pause. Exits stay open.

Tijori

A treasury contract you own. It holds USDC and tickets and lets an agent act only inside limits you set. Tijori is Hindi for safe.

Agent

A separate key, usually run by AI software, that can buy tickets and pay approved payees from your Tijori. It cannot withdraw to an address of its choosing, and you can pause or replace it.

Payee and limits

A payee is an address you approved for payments. Each has a rolling 30-day limit, and the Tijori has a daily limit across all payees.

USDC gas

On Arc, transaction fees are paid in USDC, so no second token is needed to use the app.

ApprovalWallet batch

Permission for a contract to move a set amount of your tokens. The app approves the exact amount, runs the action, then resets the approval to zero. Wallets that support batching do this in one confirmation.

TestnetFaucet funds

A practice network. Its USDC comes free from a faucet and has no real value. On Arc mainnet the USDC is real.

04

Risks

What can go wrong.

  • The vault can lose money. A fixed rate is only as safe as the vault underneath. If the vault takes a loss, tickets may pay less than face value.
  • The vault can run short of USDC. Cash out may be unavailable for a time. Taking vault shares still works.
  • Selling early depends on the pool. Before maturity you get the pool's price, which can be below what you paid.
  • Yield tokens can lose their whole cost. If the vault earns less than you paid for the yield token, you lose the difference.
  • Purchases close one hour before maturity. A ticket bought minutes before its date earns almost nothing, so new purchases stop early. Exits are never affected.
  • USDC itself can be paused or an address blocked by its issuer. USDC payouts would stop while that lasts. Taking vault shares does not move USDC and still works.
  • This is unaudited software. Nobody independent has reviewed the contracts. Each series is capped at 500 USDC. Only put in what you can afford to lose.

Who absorbs a vault loss

Every ticket is a claim on a fixed number of vault shares, not on a fixed number of dollars. If the vault loses value, each share is worth less, so every principal token of that series pays less than face value by the same proportion. It does not matter who cashes out first.

Yield tokens are never paid for a loss or for the recovery from one. They start earning again only once the vault is worth more than its previous high.

Yield after maturity

A yield token stops earning at maturity. A principal token that is not cashed out straight away keeps its vault shares, and those shares keep earning for its holder until it is cashed out.

Sudden jumps in the vault price

If the vault's share price moves by more than 1% at once, new purchases pause and yield tokens stop accruing until the move is confirmed. The allowed band widens by 0.25% for each day that passes, so a real change is accepted after a while and normal growth never trips it. Exits stay open throughout.

05

Control

Who controls what.

The contracts cannot be upgraded and have no fee switch. There is one owner address, set at deployment and never changeable.

The owner can

  • Register a new series, and attach one pool to it, once.
  • Pause new purchases, for one series or for all of them.
  • Add and remove its own liquidity in the pools.

The owner cannot

  • Move, freeze or redirect the vault shares backing any ticket.
  • Stop anyone from cashing out, merging, selling or claiming interest.
  • Change a series, its maturity date, its vault or its cap after it is registered.
  • Touch a Tijori. Each one answers only to the person who created it.

The owner is trusted for one thing: only registering series whose vault and token contracts are genuine. A dishonest or compromised owner could list a fake series, and anyone who bought into it could lose that money. Existing series would be unaffected.
