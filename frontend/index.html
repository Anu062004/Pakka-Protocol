The problem

Every rate on Arc changes daily.

Morpho might pay 5% this week and 3% next month. Fine for a saver who checks in. Not fine for an AI agent that owes 50 USDC for an API bill in exactly 30 days and has to know today, with certainty, that it will have it.

In Hindi, pakka means certain — a deal that is settled and will not change. That is the promise a fixed rate makes.

Who it's for

A saver, an agent, and the market that connects them.

Pakka works the same way for a person with idle USDC and for software managing its own budget.

Priya saves

Picks a date, signs once.

She has 100 USDC she won't need for three months. She picks 31 January, signs once, and gets a ticket that pays a known amount on that date — or she sells it early if plans change.

Rahul's agent budgets

Builds its own bond ladder.

His agent spends ~50 USDC a month on paid APIs. One call books three tickets, one per month. Each matures exactly when a bill is due, inside a Tijori the agent can't empty.

Arjun buys the variable side

Takes the other side of the trade.

He thinks Morpho's rate is going up, so he buys the yield token instead. The two sides always add up to one asset — that's what makes the fixed rate possible in the first place.

How it works

Three steps, no rate guessing.

The mechanism is Pendle's split-yield design, applied to a Morpho vault on Arc — nothing like it exists on Arc yet.

01

Split

A vault position splits into a principal ticket (PT), worth exactly 1 USDC at maturity, and a yield token (YT) that collects every bit of variable interest until then.

02

Buy at a discount

Buy PT below 1 USDC on the open market. The discount is your fixed rate — known the moment you buy, not estimated from a changing pool rate.

03

Redeem on the date

On maturity day, redeem PT for exactly 1 USDC. No rate math, no checking back — the ticket already told you the number.

Built for agents

A safe your agent can't empty.

Agents shouldn't hold a budget freely. A Tijori (Hindi for safe) is a small contract the owner deploys. The agent can act inside it — never outside it.

The agent can

  • Buy PT through the router, for registered series only
  • Cash out matured PT, claim YT interest
  • Pay owner-approved payees, within per-payee and daily caps

The agent cannot

  • Send USDC, PT or YT to any address it chooses
  • Change its own limits, payees, or the router
  • Act after the owner pauses it or replaces its key

Why Arc

USDC is the money and the gas.

An agent never needs a second, volatile token just to keep transacting — and running out of gas is a real way for a bot to stall on other chains.
